# AGOS

**A predictive, community-connected flood early-warning system for Zambales, Philippines.**

Entry for the **ASEAN Data Science Explorers 2026** competition (Philippines). The deliverable is a 15-page landscape PDF storyboard whose charts are generated in **SAP Analytics Cloud**, targeting **SDG 11** (Sustainable Cities and Communities) and **SDG 13** (Climate Action).

---

## The argument in six lines

1. Flood exposure is an ASEAN condition, not a Philippine one — **six of ten** member states score ≥8.0 on river flood hazard.
2. What separates outcomes is not hazard but **coping capacity** — a solvable variable.
3. The Philippines is **8th of 10** on river flood hazard yet **1st on all-hazard exposure**. Single-hazard indices under-count it, because Philippine flooding is compound.
4. In Zambales it is lahar-amplified: **4.7 billion m³** of Pinatubo lahar sits in the Bucao and Santo Tomas rivers, of which **1.06% has been dredged in 35 years**.
5. It was foreseeable. PAGASA's own 30-year normals already made **August Olongapo's wettest month at 865.97 mm** — 25.6% of the annual total.
6. On **6 September 2026**, nine of eleven cut-off Aeta communities in Botolan could only be reached by helicopter. The data existed. The warning did not.

## Two working rules

These exist because v1 failed on both, and they are not optional.

**1. No number without a citation row.** Source credibility is scored inside ADSE's 25% Analysis & Insights criterion. v1 carried three headline figures that did not survive checking — a national agriculture damage figure presented as one city's flood damage, an inflated national toll, and an evacuation ladder with no source at its stated scope. All three are recorded in the *Rejected* table in `SOURCES.md` so they cannot creep back in.

**2. Scope is part of the number.** Every figure is labelled national, provincial, municipal, or barangay. All three v1 failures were scope drift, not fabrication.
