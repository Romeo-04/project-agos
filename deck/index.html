AGOS
The flood warning that reaches the last house on the river.
AGOS is a predictive river-sensing and community-verified alerting system for Zambales, Philippines — turning rainfall data into hours of warning for the households that current flood indices cannot see.

ASEAN

Six of ten ASEAN states face severe river flood hazard.

This is not one country's problem, and it is not one country's solution.

  • Viet Nam 9.9, Thailand 9.8, Myanmar 8.8, Cambodia 8.6, Indonesia 8.4 and Lao PDR 8.2 all sit at or above 8 on a 0–10 scale.
  • The Philippines sits 8th of ten, at 6.7.
  • Singapore scores 0.0, the one member state the monsoon does not reach this way.
Leave the Philippines where the data puts it. Eighth looks like the wrong country to build in. The next two pages are about why that reading is wrong, and the argument only lands if the rank is shown honestly first.

ASEAN

Hazard and capacity are independent.

Where they diverge, a flood becomes a disaster.

  • The x-axis is what the weather does. The y-axis is what happens next: higher means weaker capacity to respond.
  • Quadrant lines sit at the ASEAN medians, not round numbers: flood hazard 8.3, lack of coping capacity 3.9. Both ship as columns in the dataset, so the lines come from the data.
  • The legend shows three quadrants, not four.
No ASEAN state combines high flood hazard with strong coping capacity. That empty quadrant is a real property of the region, not a rendering fault.

Philippines

8th for river floods. 1st for hazard exposure. The index is measuring the wrong thing.

  • −1.6 on river flood hazard, rank 8 of 10
  • +2.25 on all-hazard Hazard & Exposure, rank 1 of 10
  • +1.1 on composite INFORM Risk (5.3), rank 3 of 10
This inversion is the problem statement. Philippine flooding is compound (typhoon-driven, and in Zambales lahar-amplified), so a single-hazard river-flood index systematically under-counts it. If regional indices cannot see the hazard Zambales faces, local sensing and community verification are the only instrument that can.

Olongapo

August was already Olongapo's wettest month.

For thirty years running.

  • August normal 865.97 mm, ahead of July's 788.38 and September's 632.31.
  • August alone carries 25.6% of annual rainfall. June–September together carry 82.5% of a 3,385.75 mm annual normal.
  • The city's entire flood exposure is compressed into four months, and one dominates.
The August 2026 sequence. 10 Aug: 517 individuals / 165 families evacuated, 11 barangays affected. 18 Aug: 1,076 individuals / 336 families. 28–29 Aug: renewed flooding, major roads impassable, erosion at Barangay Kalaklan.

Olongapo

From rainfall to river level: converting data into hours.

  • EYE uses Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, which sees through monsoon cloud. Optical satellite is blind exactly when the flood is happening; radar is not.
  • PULSE adds non-contact ultrasonic sensors mounted above the historical maximum flood line on Del Rosario, Sta. Rita and Kalaklan. No new civil works at the pilot site.
  • Antecedent rainfall drives a river-stage forecast; the lead-time interval is what the next page prices.

Olongapo

A warning that arrives, on the channel people already have.

Three severity states, aligned to PAGASA's own rainfall-warning convention. Colour never carries the meaning alone — every state ships with an icon and a text label.

WatchRiver rising. Prepare.
WarningFlooding likely within hours. Be ready to leave.
Evacuate NowLeave for the evacuation centre immediately.
PATH carries road status from barangay-verified officers. Anything older than 2 hours auto-downgrades to Unconfirmed, so a stale green road never reads as safe. 437 road sections and 27 bridges were affected nationally in August 2026 — a warning that does not say which way out is still open is only half a warning.
NDRRMC situational reports, August 2026. Scope: Philippines, national. Severity colours follow PAGASA's rainfall-warning convention.

Zambales

What one hour of warning is worth.

  • Under current reactive warning, evacuation reaches 30% at one hour and 79% at two.
  • With AGOS lead time, the same curve reaches 80% at one hour and 100% at two.
  • The gap is not the technology. It is the head start.
This is a model, and here are its assumptions.
A1 · sourced — anchored to the Olongapo evacuation of 18 Aug 2026, 1,076 individuals / 336 families.
A2 · sourced — current warning is reactive: the alert is issued only once the river reaches evacuation level at the bridge.
A3 · estimate — AGOS gives 2 hours of lead time. Pending channel travel-time validation.
A4 · estimate — evacuation follows an S-curve; households move faster once neighbours do.

Zambales

Nine of eleven could only be reached by helicopter.

On 6 September 2026, five days before this storyboard was submitted, 11 Aeta communities in Botolan, totalling 2,519 families, were cut off by the swollen Bucao.

  • Relief took over seven hours.
  • Nine communities reached by Philippine Air Force helicopter. Two by carabao-drawn cart.
  • Earlier that week a father and his 5-year-old daughter drowned crossing to Barangay Palis.
  • Botolan holds Zambales' largest Aeta population; the 2009 Bucao dike collapse displaced over 20,000 people. The exposure is structural.
The design consequence. Because the most exposed users are the least connected, the solar-powered barangay siren is not a fallback — it is the primary channel for the highest-risk population.
Contemporaneous news reporting, 6–7 September 2026. Scope: Botolan, Zambales. Distance from the town proper is given as one 25–30 km range for the whole group and is not charted per barangay.

What breaks, and what we do about it.

Ten identified risks, scored 1–5 on likelihood and impact. The two worst are communications failures, not sensor failures.

Business model

Funded by the sand the floods leave behind.

  • Pinatubo left 4.7 billion m³ of lahar in the Zambales rivers: Bucao 3.0 bn (63.8%), Santo Tomas 1.6 bn (34.0%).
  • 50 million m³ has been dredged. That is 1.06% in 35 years.
  • Four streams: hardware, platform subscription, anonymised data-insights licensing, and grant and development finance.
At the current rate the backlog is permanent. That is the strongest available argument that the constraint is financing cadence, not engineering, and therefore that a self-funding reserve loop is the right intervention rather than a larger one-off budget request.
Two figures are deliberately absent. Lahar-sand price per m³ and sensor/subscription costs are not yet sourced, so no revenue projection is shown. A fabricated one would not survive the first Viability question.
MGB Central Luzon lahar volume estimates; Zambales River Restoration Program, running since 2023, river deltas cleared only. Scope: Zambales province.

Twenty-four months, four phases, one monsoon to prove it.

PhaseMonthsWhat happens
Engagement & Planning1–3Co-design with the LGU; MOU at provincial PDRRMO level
Deployment & Set-up4–9Sensors on three bridges; officer accounts; SAC dashboards live
Operation & Stress Test10–15First full monsoon under AGOS
Scaling & Reporting16–24Region III; annual report; ADInet feed
KPIs each carry a baseline, because a KPI without one is not measurable: sensor uptime (baseline 0 — no sensors exist) · alert lead time (0 — warning currently begins at visible flooding) · barangays with an active PATH account (0) · lahar dredged per quarter (1.06% in 35 years). Targets are blank on purpose — those are the team's numbers to commit to.
Programme design by the team. Phase boundaries are planning assumptions. Named partners: AHA Centre / ADInet · ACDM · NDRRMC-OCD · DELSA-JAIF · World Bank / ADB · DENR-MGB · DPWH · PAGASA.

The countries that need this most are the ones we can reach next.

  • The weak-capacity corner names its own targets: Myanmar 5.6, Lao PDR 5.6 and Cambodia 5.5 on lack of coping capacity, all three also above the ASEAN median for river flood hazard.
  • Same monsoon, same river-basin geometry, same gap between hazard and the ability to respond to it.
The closing move. Push Zambales data into the AHA Centre's ADInet, so a Zambales flood appears in ASEAN's own regional situational-awareness feed rather than only in a national one.
