## Discrepancy to be aware of — read before citing the 6.7 bn figure

The two reports frame the **6.7 billion m³** figure differently:

- Daily Tribune: 6.7 bn m³ is the total lahar **spewed since the 1991 eruption**, of which
  4.7 bn m³ settled in Zambales' rivers.
- BusinessMirror: MGB estimated 6.7 bn m³ of volcanic debris **remain on the Zambales side**
  of Pinatubo.

These are not the same claim. **Use the 4.7 bn river-bound figure and its breakdown**, which
both sources support and which is internally consistent (3.0 ÷ 0.638 = 4.70). Cite 6.7 bn only
with the framing of whichever source is being quoted, never as a bare number.

The `Other Zambales channels` row (0.1 bn m³ / 2.2%) is the residual after Bucao and Santo
Tomas. It is arithmetic, not a reported figure — label it as such if it appears on a chart.

## Why this matters to the storyboard

**1.06% removed in 35 years** is the single most damning figure available for the problem
section, and it is the baseline against which the FUND component's KPI is set (P13, P14).
At the current rate the backlog is effectively permanent, which is precisely why the
self-funding dredging loop is the right intervention rather than a larger one-off budget request.

It also corrects v1, which asserted "~1% removed since 2020." The share is right; the
timeframe is not — the dredging programme did not begin until 2023.
